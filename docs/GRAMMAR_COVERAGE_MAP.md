# DeutschKraft A1–B1 grammar coverage map

This is an independently authored teaching sequence aligned to the BAMF DTZ linguistic inventory and the user's A1–B1 grammar references. It is a curriculum map, not a verbatim reproduction of the BAMF handbook examples.

## 1. Verb

- A1: Präsens; personal endings; sein/haben; regular and common irregular verbs; modal verbs können/müssen/wollen/möchten; separable prefixes; imperative; infinitive position.
- A2: Perfekt with haben/sein; common Präteritum forms of sein/haben/modal verbs; reflexive verbs; verb + Akkusativ/Dativ; common verb + preposition patterns; subordinate-clause verb-final order.
- B1: expanded Präteritum and Plusquamperfekt recognition; Futur I for intention/prediction; Konjunktiv II (hätte/wäre/könnte/sollte/würde); Passiv Präsens/Präteritum/Perfekt; passive with modal verbs; expanded verb valency and prepositional complements; word-building prefixes.

## 2. Noun

- A1: gender (der/die/das), singular/plural, Nominativ and simple Akkusativ.
- A2: Dativ, possessive forms, common plural patterns, compound nouns, common nominalisations.
- B1: Genitiv recognition and common forms, n-declension, genitive with names, nominalisation, participle-derived nouns, feminine occupational forms, diminutive -chen, compound-word analysis.

## 3. Articles and pronouns

- A1: definite/indefinite articles; personal pronouns in Nominativ; basic possessive determiners; kein.
- A2: personal pronouns in Akkusativ/Dativ; demonstratives; interrogative welch-; indefinite quantifiers (einige, manche, wenige, ein paar); reflexive and reciprocal pronouns.
- B1: da(r)- and wo(r)- compounds; jemand/niemand/man/etwas/nichts/alles; pronoun order in Dativ/Akkusativ; same/different determiners (derselbe).

## 4. Adjectives and adverbs

- A1: predicate adjectives; common descriptive adjectives; basic adverbs and word order.
- A2: attributive endings after definite/indefinite articles and zero article; comparison (komparativ/superlativ); gern/lieber/am liebsten; viel/mehr/am meisten; gut/besser/am besten.
- B1: expanded adjective declension across Nominativ/Akkusativ/Dativ; adjective/adverb distinction; ordinal numbers; prefixes/suffixes (un-, -los, -bar, -wert, -lich, -ig); compound adjectives.

## 5. Prepositions

- A1: basic place/time prepositions; fixed forms with am/um/im; simple mit/zu/nach.
- A2: temporal and local prepositions; Wechselpräpositionen (Wo? Dativ / Wohin? Akkusativ); modal prepositions; verb + preposition.
- B1: prepositions by case (Akkusativ/Dativ/Genitiv); während, wegen and common formal constructions; prepositional complements; da-/wo-compounds.

## 6. Sentence and clause structure

- A1: verb-second in main clauses; yes/no and W-Fragen; negation with nicht/kein; Satzklammer with modal and separable verbs.
- A2: main + subordinate clause; weil/dass/wenn/ob; verb-final position; time/place inversion; coordinating connectors (und/aber/oder/denn); infinitive with zu in common patterns.
- B1: relative clauses; obwohl/falls/da/als/nachdem/bevor/seitdem; um ... zu / damit; so ... dass; (an)statt ... zu; double conjunctions; connectors deshalb/deswegen/darum/trotzdem with verb-second; extended word order and clause punctuation.

## 7. Word formation

- A1: common compound words and basic prefixes.
- A2: separable/inseparable prefixes; noun suffixes; feminine occupational forms.
- B1: nominalisation, participles as adjectives/nouns, adjective suffixes, prefix/suffix meaning changes, complex compounds.

## Teaching card quality contract

For each subtopic, the app should contain:
1. a short Turkish explanation and a concise rule;
2. two independently authored natural German examples with checked Turkish/English meaning;
3. a distinct diagram/image relevant to that grammar structure;
4. at least three learning questions and two unseen fill-in-the-blank questions;
5. common mistake and correction;
6. separate learning/work progress and mistake archive keys.

The BAMF inventory itself lists seven broad linguistic groups: verb, noun, article/pronouns, adjective, preposition, sentence, and word formation. It explicitly expects structures to support communicative actions, rather than being taught as isolated grammar for its own sake.
